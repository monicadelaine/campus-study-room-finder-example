# Iteration 1 Presentation & Demo

**Time:** 5 minutes for presentation + 2 minutes for live demo

The Iteration 1 presentation is a short **iteration review**. Your
slides should tell the story of what your team planned, what actually
happened, what you produced, and what you learned. GitHub is the
detailed project record; do not recreate the entire backlog in
PowerPoint.

## Required Presentation Content

Your team may organize the slides however you choose, but cover all of
the following:

-   **Project & Iteration Goal** --- Briefly remind us what the project
    does and state what the team intended to accomplish in Iteration 1.
-   **Plan vs. Actual** --- Explain what was planned, completed, added
    during the iteration, incomplete, or blocked. Explain meaningful
    deviations from the plan.
-   **Major Accomplishments** --- Highlight the most important product
    and engineering outcomes. Include important sponsor feedback when it
    affected the work.
-   **Engineering & Quality** --- Show evidence that the work is
    credible. Use the most relevant evidence for your project:
    tests/GitHub Actions, PRs, bugs, documentation, security,
    deployment, technical investigations, decisions, or limitations.
-   **Team Contributions** --- Make major areas of ownership and
    collaboration clear. These should be supported by GitHub Issues,
    PRs, commits, reviews, stand-ups, and/or the Team Contributions
    view.
-   **Lessons Learned & Next Steps** --- Identify important product,
    technical, or process learning and explain how it affects Iteration
    2.
-   **AI Use** --- Identify significant AI use and how the resulting
    work was verified. If there was no significant AI use, state that
    briefly.

## Live Demo --- 2 Minutes

Demonstrate the most important **working, integrated functionality**
available at the end of Iteration 1.

-   Have the application and demo state ready before your presentation
    begins.
-   Show working behavior rather than describing planned functionality.
-   The demo should reflect the submitted Iteration 1 repository state.
-   If an important limitation exists, state it rather than implying the
    functionality is complete.

## Slide & Speaker Requirements

-   Put **`Speaker: First Last`** at the bottom of every slide.
-   If two people genuinely share a slide, list both speakers.
-   All team members should participate meaningfully.
-   Use GitHub screenshots or links when they provide useful evidence;
    do not reproduce detailed GitHub records in the slides.
-   Keep slides readable, concise, visually useful, and professionally
    organized.
-   Stay within the **5-minute presentation** limit.

## What to Have Ready in GitHub

Before presenting, make sure the GitHub record supports what you say:

-   Current Iteration view reflects the actual Iteration 1 results.
-   Completed Issues have appropriate evidence and meet the team's
    Definition of Done.
-   Incomplete and blocked work remains visible.
-   Significant PRs, tests/workflows, documentation, investigations, and
    decisions are linked where appropriate.
-   Team contribution evidence is current.
-   The Iteration 1 submission tag identifies the submitted repository
    state.

## A Useful Story to Tell

**Goal → Plan → Actual Results → Evidence → Learning → What Changes
Next**

The slides summarize that story. GitHub provides the detailed evidence.
The live demo shows the product.
